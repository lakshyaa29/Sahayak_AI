**SIH26092 – AI-Driven Scheme Matching for Marginalized Entrepreneurs:** The Government aims to promote the socio-economic empowerment of the Scheduled Caste (SC) population by providing concessional financial assistance and educational loans to eligible beneficiaries, particularly individuals with an annual family income of up to ₹5.00 lakh, through a range of tailored financial products that can cover up to 90% of eligible project or education costs at concessional interest rates, generally ranging from around 6.5% to 8% per annum for many products and up to approximately 15% depending on the specific scheme; however, beneficiaries cannot submit loan applications directly, because the financial assistance is delivered through a **Channel Finance System** consisting of more than 100 authorized Channel Partners, including **State Channelizing Agencies (SCAs), Public Sector Banks (PSBs), Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), and NBFC-MFIs**, which creates a major last-mile accessibility problem for the intended beneficiaries. Citizens often have limited awareness of the available schemes, the eligibility conditions associated with them, and which specific financial product best suits their individual requirements; for example, a beneficiary may struggle to determine whether a relatively small self-employment project should be financed through a **Micro Finance Scheme, which may support projects up to approximately ₹1.40 lakh**, whether a larger entrepreneurial project should be considered under a **Term Loan Scheme, which may support projects up to approximately ₹50.00 lakh**, or whether their requirement falls under an **Educational Loan Scheme**. This lack of clear, personalized guidance results in beneficiaries having to manually interpret multiple financial products and eligibility conditions based on factors such as their project or activity type, estimated project or education cost, annual family income, education status, financing requirement, and other applicable scheme criteria. Even after identifying a potentially suitable scheme, beneficiaries often do not clearly understand the financial implications of the loan, including the permissible loan amount, maximum scheme limit, percentage of project cost that may be financed, applicable interest rate, repayment period, projected Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI), and the effect of scheme-specific moratorium periods, which can typically range from approximately 3 to 12 months. A further major challenge arises because the applicant must locate and approach the correct authorized Channel Partner capable of processing the specific loan category, and simply identifying the geographically nearest bank or agency is not sufficient, since the partner must also be eligible to process the required scheme and should satisfy current fund-utilization conditions; applications should not be routed toward Channel Partners that are unsuitable because of excessive overdues, high Non-Performing Assets (NPAs), poor fund utilization, or other operational eligibility restrictions. This fragmented system causes offline confusion, repeated visits to banks or agencies, incorrect scheme selection, misrouted applications, unnecessary travel and paperwork, delays in processing and disbursement, poor beneficiary experience, and inefficient utilization of government funds. Therefore, the Government expects participants to develop a **comprehensive, intelligent, multilingual digital platform or mobile application that acts as a bridge between eligible beneficiaries and the existing Channel Finance ecosystem**, with three primary solution components. First, the platform must include a **Smart Scheme Recommender**, implemented through AI, rule-based logic, or a hybrid approach, that accepts basic beneficiary inputs such as **project type, estimated project cost, annual family income, education status, and other relevant eligibility information**, evaluates these inputs against available scheme rules, and automatically recommends the most suitable credit or educational loan scheme instead of forcing the applicant to manually compare different financial products. Second, the solution must include a **dynamic Financial Calculator** capable of producing projected financial estimates based on the recommended scheme and its specific guidelines, including applicable maximum loan limits, permissible financing amount, scheme-specific interest rates that may vary approximately from **6.5% to 15% depending on the product**, repayment duration, moratorium periods of roughly **3 to 12 months**, and projected EMI or repayment obligations, so that beneficiaries can understand the financial consequences of the loan before proceeding and thereby improve their financial literacy regarding concessional lending. Third, the solution must include a **Geo-Spatial Channel Partner Locator and Intelligent Router** integrated with an appropriate mapping service, which uses the beneficiary’s current or entered location to identify nearby authorized Channel Partners, verifies whether each partner is capable of processing the relevant financing category, checks the partner’s current fund-utilization eligibility and operational suitability, avoids routing beneficiaries to Channel Partners with problematic levels of NPAs or overdues, and recommends the nearest or most appropriate eligible SCA, PSB, RRB, or NBFC-MFI capable of handling the applicant’s specific scheme. The entire platform should be designed to reduce the complexity faced by beneficiaries, support interaction in multiple languages, make information regarding government concessional lending easier to understand, improve transparency in scheme selection and financial calculations, reduce wrongly routed applications, connect beneficiaries to the correct authorized financial institution more efficiently, reduce unnecessary offline confusion and processing delays, improve the speed and efficiency of loan disbursement, and enable better utilization of available government funds across the Channel Finance ecosystem. Ultimately, the desired system should take a beneficiary from the initial stage of **“I need financial or educational assistance but do not know which scheme applies to me, how much I may receive, what I will have to repay, or where I should go”** to a clear, actionable outcome containing **the most suitable scheme, the relevant financial calculation and repayment estimate, and the nearest currently eligible authorized Channel Partner**, thereby improving both beneficiary-level financial literacy and the overall transparency, efficiency, accessibility, routing accuracy, and fund utilization of the government’s concessional financing system.
